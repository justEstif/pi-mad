# Sharpen Mode

When the user brings a single message that needs to land — landing copy, a pitch, a slide, a stakeholder email, feedback, bad news, a negotiation opener, an apology, or outreach — skip the full run and do one audience-first sharpening pass. The burden of clarity is entirely on the communicator: when someone doesn't get it, the message failed, not the audience.

1. **Name the one person** most likely to feel immediate relief when this lands, and the frustration they felt the moment before they'd reach for this. One sentence, in their words. (Personas with name/age/title are useless for messaging.)
2. **Find the gap.** Lay two columns: what you're saying (what it does/is, your framing, your vocabulary) versus what they need to hear (what problem it solves for them, their framing, their vocabulary). The distance between the columns is why it isn't landing. A blank right column is the diagnosis.
3. **Reshape** with PAS: Problem — name the pain they already feel; Agitation — show you understand how it manifests (makes them feel seen); Solution — your thing, now that they're ready to hear it. Layer in as needed: Value (what's in it for them), Unique value (what only this does), Point of view (what you believe that others don't). A landing headline needs Value; a skeptical pitch needs Unique value; a colleague message needs neither.
4. **Test for recognition.** Could someone with that pain recognize themselves in the first sentence? Could they repeat back what it does after one read? Does anything require inside knowledge? Any no — return to step 2.

**Message kinds map onto the story library** where natural: a pitch is the `pitch-narrative` arc (problem → solution → proof → action), a brand or landing rewrite leans on `brand-story` (origin spark, values, differentiation), a case study or customer win is `customer-journey` (before/after transformation), and a data-backed message is `data-storytelling` (context → revelation → so-what → action). Pull the framework's key questions from `story-types.csv` when the user wants depth rather than a quick pass.

Interpersonal drafts (feedback, bad news, negotiation, apology, outreach): offer 2–3 strategies with different trade-offs and let the sender choose — never a single draft; never the agent's voice — match the sender's demonstrated tone and vocabulary; warmth through specificity, never filler ("hope you're doing well" before a termination email is worse than none).
